Methodology

What the numbers are, and what they are not.

What the board ranks

Each team plays each of the other 31 teams once, on a neutral field. Its score is the average of those 31 win chances. Expected wins on that hypothetical schedule are the sum, which is the average times 31. Sort by the average, then by average neutral point margin, then by team name. Ranks are 1 through 32 from that full list.

Conference, division, and search filters only hide rows. A team's rank is the full-league rank and does not change when the list gets shorter. Display rounding does not decide ties: the sort uses the unrounded average.

This is the same summary shape as College Football Index's neutral round robin. It is not a prediction of the team's real schedule, and it is not a blend of several rating weights.

From EPA margin to a point spread

Team strength is the schedule-adjusted EPA margin described below (v2 = adj_off − adj_def, EPA per play). For a neutral game the point spread is:

neutral margin = 48.67 × (v2_for − v2_against)

48.67 is the points associated with a 1.0 EPA/play gap. It is in the neighborhood of a game's offensive play count, and it is estimated rather than assumed. Figures on this page are rounded to the hundredth; conversion.json keeps more digits, and the board uses that file. A real home game adds one league home-field number:

home margin = neutral margin + 1.78

Home field is one number for the whole league, not a team effect. The ranking does not add it. Every hypothetical game on the board is neutral, so a venue cannot move a team up or down. The row detail still shows what the average margin would be if that team hosted every opponent (neutral margin + 1.78).

How those two numbers were fit

Both come from an ordinary least-squares fit on nflverse regular-season finals, seasons 2016–2025, 2639 games (10 ties, 46 neutral-site games):

home score − away score
  = 1.78 × (1 if the game is at the home stadium, else 0)
  + 48.67 × (v2_home − v2_away)
  + error

Each season's v2 is that season's full regular-season rating, the same definition the board uses. Neutral-site games (nflverse location = Neutral, mostly international) stay in the slope with home field set to zero. A missing location counts as a home game. Final scores include overtime. The fit's R² is 0.317. The residual standard deviation is 11.74 points.

Home teams won 54.7% of the decisive games in that sample. The fitted spread's average home win probability on those games is 54.9%.

Assumption to keep in mind: the rating is fit on the same games it is then asked to describe. That is the right conversion if the question is "what point spread does this season-long EPA margin correspond to?" It is not a pre-game forecast, and it is not a backtest. The weekly data refresh does not refit these coefficients. They live in nflrankings/conversion.json.

Win chance

P(win) = 1 / (1 + exp(−point margin / 7.04))

The scale 7.04 is the logistic scale fit by maximum likelihood on the same games, using the fitted point spread. Ties stay in the point regression with margin 0 and stay out of the win/loss likelihood. A 0-point spread is a 50% chance. A 3-point margin is a 60.5% win chance. That scale also lines up with the 11.74-point residual: a normal residual converts to a logistic scale of about 0.55 times the standard deviation. This is an analytic logistic, not a Monte Carlo draw of scores.

The published percentage is the average of the win chances against the other 31 teams. That is not the win chance against an average team. Those two numbers differ because the logistic is curved. The order does not differ: a higher v2 produces a higher average win chance against this shared field, so the rank column and the EPA order agree. v2 stays on the board so the EPA margin is still visible.

Schedule-adjusted EPA

Expected points added (EPA) per play comes from nflverse play-by-play. For each team we take every regular-season scrimmage play (pass or run, not a two-point try, with both teams set) with a non-null EPA, and compute:

  • Raw offensive EPA: play-weighted mean EPA on the team's offensive plays.
  • Raw defensive EPA: play-weighted mean EPA the team allowed. Lower is better.

We then adjust for opponents over 20 iterations. Each iteration, a team's offense is its raw EPA minus the play-weighted mean adjusted defensive EPA of the defenses it faced; its defense is raw EPA allowed minus the play-weighted mean adjusted offensive EPA of the offenses it faced. After every iteration both sides are re-centered so the league mean of the adjusted values equals the league mean of the raw values. The rating is v2 = adj_off − adj_def, in EPA per play; higher is better. Opening a row shows the raw and adjusted EPA figures.

Penalty-only rows are not scrimmage plays. The current nflverse file has no separate no_play column; if that column is present, those rows are dropped too.

One game, and a team's schedule

The matchup page turns two teams and a venue into a point spread, a win chance, a projected score, and an 80% margin range. The range is the fitted residual, about 11.74 points, treated as a normal distribution around the spread. A 10th-to-90th percentile is the same summary a Monte Carlo draw from that residual would estimate. It is not a separate random model, and it does not simulate drives.

Projected points use each team's points scored and allowed per game, shrunk toward the league with four prior games, then averaged the way a two-team total is averaged. Pass and rush EPA are schedule-adjusted on those play types alone. They are shown so a matchup can be read. They are not added into the spread.

A team page lists the regular-season schedule, the current-rating spread for each game, expected wins on that schedule, and a projected record that keeps results already played and adds the win chances of games not yet played. The chart is cumulative schedule-adjusted EPA after each week. These schedule numbers use the rating published for the whole season, so a game already played is described by a rating that includes it.

Frozen pregame picks

The week board and the accuracy page use a different number. Before week w, the live rating is fit only on regular-season plays from earlier weeks. When the previous season's final ratings are on file, each team is a blend of that live rating and last year's rating pulled toward the league. Last year's margin keeps 17/45 of its gap to zero, so an outlier is not a full head start. The weight on the live rating is games played so far, divided by games played plus 14. Week 1 has no live plays, so the pick is that shrunk prior plus home field. After 14 games the two parts are equal, and the live rating is the larger part after that. Points per game still use only earlier games, shrunk toward the league rate of those games. With no earlier plays and no prior season, every team is at zero and the spread is home field alone (about 1.78 points, or zero at a neutral site). That pick is stored with the game and is not refit after the game is played.

Winner hit rate ignores ties and exact 50/50 picks. Brier score is the average squared error of the pregame home win chance on the same games. Wins above expected, on the rankings board, is actual wins minus the sum of those pregame win chances. Schedule strength is the average final EPA margin of opponents already played.

Final scores on the week board are the nflverse schedule. This site does not poll a live score feed. A small score worker can be added later; until then, finals show up when the rankings file is refreshed.

This week's games

The week page opens on the earliest week that still has a game without a final score. If every game has a final, it opens on the last week with plays (week_max).

When a game has a kickoff date, its football week runs from Wednesday through the following Tuesday. Wednesday starts that week. Tuesday ends it. The date is the schedule's calendar date, not a UTC shift of a timestamp. The control still uses the nflverse week number. The label is that Wednesday–Tuesday span when every dated game in the week falls inside it. A file that only has integer weeks keeps those numbers, and the page says the dates are missing. College Football Index picks one Chicago calendar day for today's games. It does not define this NFL week.

Division and conference power

Separate from the EPA rank, played games also fit a point rating for each team, each division, and each conference. The final margin is capped at 28 points. The league home field (1.78 points, or zero at a neutral site) is then removed. Team ratings shrink toward their division. Division ratings shrink toward their conference. The two conferences shrink toward zero. A team with no division is not pooled, and an independent conference stays at zero. The current league has none.

The team prior is 2 games, the same per-team prior College Football Index uses when it fits conference strength together with team ratings. The division prior is 0.5 team, not 1. Copying the college value of 1 would pull a four-team division much harder than that prior pulls a college conference. The conference prior is 1 division toward zero. Those two group priors are defaults. They need a pregame holdout before they are added to a spread. The rankings board does not use them. The matchup score on this site is still the EPA spread.

The matchup page shows division power, conference power, and a division and conference adjustment. That adjustment is the change in the margin when the hierarchical ratings replace team ratings fit from the same scores with no division or conference pool. It can be nonzero for two teams in one division, because each team has its own schedule. College Football Index shows that kind of number as a conference adjustment and does not publish a conference table. This site also lists the eight divisions and two conferences under the rankings.

Rates that were measured and left out

Scrimmage EPA already knows the down, the distance, and the field position of each play. It does not include kickoffs, punts, field goals, or extra points, and it does not keep a separate credit for where the drive started. Those two, plus success rate, explosiveness (a pass of 20 yards or a run of 10), and points per drive, were opponent-adjusted with the same routine as EPA and blended with the previous season the same way. The point weight for each rate was the average of the season-by-season fits on 2018–2023 walk-forward games. 2024 and 2025 were the check, and 2026 was not used to pick. None of the added rates raised the winner hit rate on both check seasons. Special-teams EPA was the only added rate whose leftover correlation with the final margin was positive in every season from 2018 through 2024, and that weight still lost winners. The same replay also scores a prior that fades after 8 or 12 games instead of 14. The 14-game blend still had the most winners across 2018–2024. The spread stays on scrimmage EPA. scripts/screen_advanced.py replays this from the nflverse release files.

On 2018–2025 regular-season games this pick was right in 1,352 of 2,119 decisive games (63.8 percent, Brier 0.226). The nflverse closing line on those same games, which is not an input, was right in 1,407 of 2,119 (66.4 percent). Through 2026 week 8 the pick is 73 of 120 (60.8 percent, Brier 0.234): 40 of 64 in weeks 1–4 (62.5 percent) and 33 of 56 in weeks 5–8 (58.9 percent). The line on that same 2026 set is 79 of 120 (65.8 percent). College Football Index’s figure near 80 percent counts college games, including large mismatches. NFL games do not separate that cleanly, and the line itself is in the mid-60s. Who is playing, and the rest of the open file, is scored in the next section.

Every open aspect, with weights that change

The five rates above each got one fixed weight. This pass puts the rest of the open nflverse file in one model and lets the weights change by season. For a game in season Y the correction is a ridge on the point residual of the scrimmage-EPA spread. Training games are the previous four seasons only. A season two years back counts half as much as the most recent of those four. The ridge penalty is chosen by walking forward inside that window, so the target season is outside the fit. There is no intercept: home field is already in the spread, and every feature is a home-minus-away gap. 2024 and 2025 are the check. 2026 is reported and was not used to choose the window, the penalty grid, or the feature list.

The features are opponent-adjusted matchups from plays before the week, blended with last season the same way as EPA. They cover pass and rush EPA, early and late downs, the red zone, snaps whose pre-play win probability is between 10 and 90 percent, success rate, explosiveness, yards per play, turnovers, sacks, quarterback hits, penalty yards, drive starts, points per drive, scoring drives, drives that avoid a turnover, and special-teams EPA. Pass and rush are also weighted by how often each offense passes or runs. The schedule adds rest, travel, wind, and cold. Personnel adds the expected quarterback's career pass EPA, shrunk with 300 prior plays and built from passer rows back to 1999, and the difference in players ruled out. The expected starter is the first offensive-chart quarterback who is not out or doubtful. From 2025 the chart is the last daily snapshot before the game date. Earlier seasons use that week's chart. An injury timestamp after the game date is dropped. That chart matched the schedule's listed starter on 5,092 of 5,520 team-games (92.2 percent).

Snap counts stay out because they are a postgame tally, and last week's snaps are already inside the next week's EPA. The closing spread, moneyline, and total stay out of the fit. The spread is only the comparison above. In this schedule file a positive spread_line means the home team is favored. Coach and referee names stay out, because a flag per person would memorize clubs. Oakland's rating is not handed to Las Vegas, and San Diego's is not handed to the Chargers. The EPA prior makes the same break.

On the walk-forward games the dynamic model was 1,007 of 1,576 in 2018–2023 (63.9 percent, Brier 0.226), against 997 of 1,576 for scrimmage EPA (63.3 percent, Brier 0.227). The check went the other way. 2024 was 182 of 272 (66.9 percent, Brier 0.212) against 183 of 272 (67.3 percent, Brier 0.215). 2025 was 168 of 271 (62.0 percent, Brier 0.233) against 172 of 271 (63.5 percent, Brier 0.231). 2026, held out of the fit, is 75 of 120 (62.5 percent, Brier 0.223): 38 of 64 in weeks 1–4 and 37 of 56 from week 5 on. Scrimmage EPA on that same 2026 set remains 73 of 120, with Brier 0.234. The published spread stays on scrimmage EPA.

The 2024 weights were fit on 2020–2023, with the heavier penalty the inner walk-forward picked. The 2025 weights were fit on 2021–2024. Averaged across those two fits, the largest weights were the quarterback (0.35 points per standard deviation), players ruled out (0.32), and late-down EPA (0.25). On the 2021–2023 walk-forward, taking out success rate, explosiveness, and yards per play left the winner count unchanged at 517 of 812, and taking out special teams dropped it to 504. That is a description of the fit window. The check kept the full list. An equal-weight ridge frozen on 2018–2023 scored 185 of 272 in 2024 and 165 of 271 in 2025, so one blend of the same features does not hold up either. scripts/screen_aspects.py replays the screen. The weights and the season table are in nflrankings/aspect_screen.json.

What this is not

It is not DVOA. Injury reports, weather, and garbage-time snaps were measured in the aspect screen and are not in the spread. It is not a betting model. Home field is not team-specific.

The margin range does not simulate drives. Pass and rush EPA are not a second spread model. Live in-game scores are not polled.

Data

All data is free: nflverse play-by-play, schedules, and team metadata, fetched with nfl_data_py. Nothing is scraped. Records and point differential come from played regular-season games only. Logos prefer team_logo_espn, then team_logo_wikipedia, and only https URLs are kept. Colors are team_color and team_color2 when they are hex colors.
