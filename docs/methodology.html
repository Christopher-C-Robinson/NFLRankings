Methodology

What the numbers are, and what they are not.

← Rankings

What the board ranks

Each team plays each of the other 31 teams once, on a neutral field. Its score is the average of those 31 win chances. Expected wins on that hypothetical schedule are the sum, which is the average times 31. Sort by the average, then by average neutral point margin, then by team name. Ranks are 1 through 32 from that full list.

Conference, division, and search filters only hide rows. A team's rank is the full-league rank and does not change when the list gets shorter. Display rounding does not decide ties: the sort uses the unrounded average.

This is the same summary shape as College Football Index's neutral round robin. It is not a prediction of the team's real schedule, and it is not a blend of several rating weights.

From EPA margin to a point spread

Team strength is the schedule-adjusted EPA margin described below (v2 = adj_off − adj_def, EPA per play). For a neutral game the point spread is:

neutral margin = 48.67 × (v2_for − v2_against)

48.67 is the points associated with a 1.0 EPA/play gap. It is in the neighborhood of a game's offensive play count, and it is estimated rather than assumed. Figures on this page are rounded to the hundredth; conversion.json keeps more digits, and the board uses that file. A real home game adds one league home-field number:

home margin = neutral margin + 1.78

Home field is one number for the whole league, not a team effect. The ranking does not add it. Every hypothetical game on the board is neutral, so a venue cannot move a team up or down. The row detail still shows what the average margin would be if that team hosted every opponent (neutral margin + 1.78).

How those two numbers were fit

Both come from an ordinary least-squares fit on nflverse regular-season finals, seasons 2016–2025, 2639 games (10 ties, 46 neutral-site games):

home score − away score
  = 1.78 × (1 if the game is at the home stadium, else 0)
  + 48.67 × (v2_home − v2_away)
  + error

Each season's v2 is that season's full regular-season rating, the same definition the board uses. Neutral-site games (nflverse location = Neutral, mostly international) stay in the slope with home field set to zero. A missing location counts as a home game. Final scores include overtime. The fit's R² is 0.317. The residual standard deviation is 11.74 points.

Home teams won 54.7% of the decisive games in that sample. The fitted spread's average home win probability on those games is 54.9%.

Assumption to keep in mind: the rating is fit on the same games it is then asked to describe. That is the right conversion if the question is "what point spread does this season-long EPA margin correspond to?" It is not a pre-game forecast, and it is not a backtest. The weekly data refresh does not refit these coefficients. They live in nflrankings/conversion.json.

Win chance

P(win) = 1 / (1 + exp(−point margin / 7.04))

The scale 7.04 is the logistic scale fit by maximum likelihood on the same games, using the fitted point spread. Ties stay in the point regression with margin 0 and stay out of the win/loss likelihood. A 0-point spread is a 50% chance. A 3-point margin is a 60.5% win chance. That scale also lines up with the 11.74-point residual: a normal residual converts to a logistic scale of about 0.55 times the standard deviation. This is an analytic logistic, not a Monte Carlo draw of scores.

The published percentage is the average of the win chances against the other 31 teams. That is not the win chance against an average team. Those two numbers differ because the logistic is curved. The order does not differ: a higher v2 produces a higher average win chance against this shared field, so the rank column and the EPA order agree. v2 stays on the board so the EPA margin is still visible.

Schedule-adjusted EPA

Expected points added (EPA) per play comes from nflverse play-by-play. For each team we take every regular-season scrimmage play (pass or run, not a two-point try, with both teams set) with a non-null EPA, and compute:

  • Raw offensive EPA: play-weighted mean EPA on the team's offensive plays.
  • Raw defensive EPA: play-weighted mean EPA the team allowed. Lower is better.

We then adjust for opponents over 20 iterations. Each iteration, a team's offense is its raw EPA minus the play-weighted mean adjusted defensive EPA of the defenses it faced; its defense is raw EPA allowed minus the play-weighted mean adjusted offensive EPA of the offenses it faced. After every iteration both sides are re-centered so the league mean of the adjusted values equals the league mean of the raw values. The rating is v2 = adj_off − adj_def, in EPA per play; higher is better. Opening a row shows the raw and adjusted EPA figures.

Penalty-only rows are not scrimmage plays. The current nflverse file has no separate no_play column; if that column is present, those rows are dropped too.

What this is not

It is not DVOA. It does not add an injury, weather, or garbage-time adjustment beyond whatever is already inside nflverse EPA. It is not a betting model. Special teams are not given their own term. Home field is not team-specific.

Not on this board yet: a matchup simulator, team pages, a week-by-week games view, live scores, and an accuracy backtest. predict_matchup already turns two ratings and a venue into one spread and one win chance for a later matchup view. It does not simulate draws.

Data

All data is free: nflverse play-by-play, schedules, and team metadata, fetched with nfl_data_py. Nothing is scraped. Records and point differential come from played regular-season games only. Logos prefer team_logo_espn, then team_logo_wikipedia, and only https URLs are kept. Colors are team_color and team_color2 when they are hex colors.
